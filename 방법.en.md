# Methods — TMTF Test

[한국어](방법.md) · **English**

This document is for clinicians and researchers who send the test. Instructions for participants are in [README.en.md](README.en.md).

## 1. Stimuli

| Item | Value | Source · code |
|---|---|---|
| Carrier | Broadband Gaussian noise, flat from 20 Hz to half the sampling rate. Newly generated for each interval | `js/stim.js` `noise()` |
| Modulation | s(t) = [1 + m·sin(2π·fm·t + φ)]·n(t), m = 10^(D/20). D = modulation depth (dB), 0 dB = 100% | Viemeister 1979 |
| Modulation starting phase φ | Random between 0 and 2π for each interval | Shen & Richards 2013 |
| Level | After the ramps are applied, the RMS of each interval is normalized directly to 0.05 (−26.02 dBFS) → modulated and unmodulated sounds have exactly the same average level | Viemeister 1979 Appendix (principle of the `√(1 + m²/2)` correction) · Shen & Richards 2013 · `tools/lean/TMTF/Level.lean` |
| Onset · offset | 10 ms linear ramps | Won et al. 2011 · Park et al. 2015 |
| Timing | 0.5 s sound × 3, 0.5 s gaps, 0.25 s of silence before the first sound | Three 0.5 s intervals + 0.5 s gaps: De Ruiter et al. 2015 · leading silence: Goehring & Carlyon 2026 (AUDITO, 200 ms example) |
| Loudness | The participant sets a comfortable level using unmodulated noise in the same band, and the level is then kept fixed. dB SPL is not measured | |
| Reproducibility | The noise seed (mulberry32) of each interval is recorded → the exact same sound can be regenerated | `tools/verify_result.py` |

## 2. Task and staircase

- **Task:** Three-interval oddity (pick the one that differs).
  - While each sound plays, the box with its number lights up. The light is timed to when the sound actually leaves the output device.
  - Responses are accepted after all three sounds have finished.
- **Not sure:** Counted as a random choice among the three positions. This is an unbiased guess that does not favor any particular number.
- **Staircase:** 2-down 1-up (Levitt 1971, Table I Entry 2), converging on the 70.7%-correct point.
  - Starts at 0 dB (100%). Steps are 4 dB through the 1st–4th reversals, and 2 dB from right after the 4th reversal through the 5th–14th reversals (10 reversals). The depth range is −40 to 0 dB.
  - The track ends after 14 reversals, and the threshold is the arithmetic mean of the last 10 (an even number) (the rule of Won et al. 2011 · Park et al. 2015).
- **Not measured:** De Ruiter et al. (2015) assigned 0 dB when modulation could not be detected, but did not specify a stopping count. This app sets its own rule, stopping immediately after 4 errors at 0 dB, and reports the result as “Not measured” rather than a number, excluding it from averages (for the limits of staircases near 100% modulation, see Viemeister 1979 Appendix).
- **At chance:** When there are 6 or more trials at 0 dB, the app checks whether a one-sided binomial test gives P(X ≥ k | n, 1/3) > 0.05.
- **Incomplete:** The track stops when it reaches 160 trials. That value is for reference only.
- **Feedback:** Correct / incorrect is shown after every trial.

This procedure is the same as the ‘custom’ (직접 정하기) condition of the 와우TMTF (CI-TMTF) app.

```
직접·3구간·광대역·0.5초·쉼0.5초·흔들기없음·반전14·마지막10·직선경사
```

(custom · 3 intervals · broadband · 0.5 s · 0.5 s gap · no roving · 14 reversals · last 10 · linear ramps)

- This name is stored in `procKey` in the result file.
- Because this app combines validated elements from several papers, its procedure is not identical to that of any single paper.
  - **Won et al. (2011) · Park et al. (2015):** 2-interval 2AFC, 1 s + 1 s intervals joined without a gap, no phase term (`φ = 0`), sound-field presentation at 65 dBA.
  - **De Ruiter et al. (2015):** The same three-interval oddity task (0.5 s + 0.5 s), but with a 4th-order band-pass filter (`80–8500 Hz`), 30 ms linear ramps, a `−2 dB` starting point, the mean of the last 6 of 8 reversals (4 → 2 dB, switching after 2 reversals), and sound-field presentation at 65 dB SPL.
  - **Viemeister (1979):** Continuous broadband noise carrier (§IV reports that gated carriers can show a high-pass segment at low frequencies).
  - **Shen & Richards (2013):** 4-interval 2AFC (target in interval 2 or 3), 10 ms raised-cosine ramps, fixed 50-trial staircase (8 → 5 → 2 dB).
- Therefore, results from this app are not ranked directly against the group values reported in these papers.

## 3. Session

0. **Keep-alive signal · short sound check (four beeps):**
   - **18 Hz keep-alive signal (`tone18all`, default):** When the first sound plays on the volume-setting screen, an 18 Hz sine wave (peak −45 dBFS, amplitude 0.005623, RMS −48 dBFS) is output continuously from `master`. It is not mixed into the stimulus buffers (`js/stim.js`), so the stimulus and staircase verification (JS = Python · Lean 4) remains valid. Once turned on, it is not turned off even after the session or check ends, and right after it is first turned on, the app waits 3 seconds before the first sound. Participants can turn it off with the **Turn steady signal off** button if they wish.
   - **Short sound check (four beeps):** After the volume setting and before practice, a four-harmonic complex tone with a 1 kHz fundamental (1 · 2 · 3 · 4 kHz, amplitude 1/h, 120 ms, 20 ms raised-cosine ramps at both ends, RMS −24 dBFS, 130 ms spacing, 4 tones totaling 1,030 ms) is presented **twice, each after 3 seconds of leading silence**. The participant is asked how many beeps they heard (0–4) and whether the first one sounded softer or shorter (yes · no · not sure); the check can be skipped. If the highest rate of missing beeps or a weak first beep at any silence length is 34% or more, the verdict is “suspected onset clipping”; if it is lower but some beeps were missed or weak, “occasional misses”; otherwise, “normal”.
1. **Practice ×3:** At 100% modulation, using the lowest of the selected frequencies.
   - Practice is not included in the results, but every trial is recorded.
   - It can be repeated if the participant wishes.
2. **One round:** Each selected frequency is measured once, in random order.
   - The defaults are the 7 standard Won et al. (2011) frequencies: **10 · 50 · 75 · 100 · 150 · 200 · 300 Hz**, presented in random order for each participant. *(Reference: the original laboratory study presented broadband noise at 65 dBA to measure modulation detection thresholds [MDT]; in a web browser, sound pressure is uncalibrated, so participants set a comfortable volume.)*
   - They can be changed with `#hz=` in the link (see the README).
3. **Save & resume later:** If a participant feels fatigued, tapping **Save & resume later** on the break screen saves completed runs and the remaining plan locally so they can continue later via **Resume test** on the home screen (`session-pause` / `session-resume` events are recorded).
4. **One more round:** When the round ends, the app offers another round of the same frequencies in a new order.
5. **One more run only for large differences:** If both rounds produced numeric values that differ by more than 3 dB, the app offers one more run of only that frequency (Won et al. 2011 Fig. 9 · Park et al. 2015).
6. **Other:**
   - A break screen appears between runs.
   - Pressing **Pause** discards that run, and the same frequency is restarted from the beginning. The trials of the discarded run remain in the record.
   - If the screen is hidden while a sound is playing, that trial is not scored, and the same depth is played again when the participant returns.
   - On supported browsers, the screen is kept from turning off during the test.

## 4. Records (result file)

The result file is JSON (`format: "ci-tmtf-public"`, `formatVersion: 2`; older `formatVersion: 1` files can still be read), and it contains the following for each session.

- **Session**
  - Start and end times, and the reason it ended
  - ID, ear, listening method, volume (if entered)
  - Optional info (age group · years of use · onset of hearing loss · sound processor (external part of the cochlear implant) manufacturer · note; `null` if not chosen)
  - Procedure `P`, condition name `procKey`, plan and rounds
  - Practice trials, setup log (times of sound playback · listening to examples · volume confirmation)
  - Keep-alive signal (`keepalive`: mode · whether it was turned off manually · start time · initial 3 s wait · switch log)
  - Four-beep check (`onsetCheck`: whether skipped · leading silence · per-trial number of beeps heard · first-beep answer · response time · replays · keep-alive state at the time · verdict summary)
  - Browser · screen · audio (sampling rate, output latency, keep-alive mode)
  - Build hashes and verification status, end-of-test question (only if it was shown)
- **Events**
  - Run start · end, breaks, screen hidden and returned
  - Trials discarded because the screen was hidden (including the sound that was playing), the pause dialog text and answer
  - Discarded runs (all trials · reversals · current depth), ignored keys
  - Output dropouts (start · end · reason · duration), one-more-round offers and answers
- **Runs**
  - Threshold (dB), reversal SD, not measured, number of 0 dB trials and correct answers, p, at chance, incomplete
  - Reversals and the trials where they occurred, number of trials, number of “Not sure” answers, duration, preceding break time
- **Trials**
  - Depth, target position, response position, correct, not sure, input (key · click), response time
  - Per-interval seed · RMS · peak, modulation phase, scheduling margin, lateness, output latency, light timing offset
  - Screen hidden, number of replays, keys pressed while a sound was playing (early input)
  - Time elapsed since the output was last confirmed

**Checking the actual 18 Hz output and continuity:** Once audio is first turned on, it is never closed. Every second during the session or check, a Hann-windowed single-bin DFT at 18 Hz on the analyser placed right before the output (32,768 samples) checks the actual output level (−45 ± 1.5 dBFS), the audio state (`running`), and the clock (1 ± 0.05 relative to real time). Any deviation is logged with its time, reason, and duration.

**Where data is stored:** All stimulus generation, adaptive staircases, records, reports, and opening result files are strictly designed to run only inside the browser (including the public GitHub Pages site), and no cookies, analytics tools, external CDNs, or server transfers are used. Records are kept only in that device browser's `localStorage` (`ciTmtf.v1`), a file is created only when the participant taps **Save result file**, and on a shared device the records can be erased immediately with **Delete local records** on the home screen. For participant ID links, using a URL hash (`#id=P02`), which is not included in HTTP requests, is recommended. Because security cannot be perfect in every environment, we will keep checking for vulnerabilities and continue to release security updates.

## 5. Interpretation · research and legal notes

- **For research and education only (not a medical device):** This tool was made for research and educational purposes and is not a medical device (SaMD) under the Republic of Korea's Medical Devices Act or Digital Medical Products Act, or under foreign standards (FDA · EU MDR). It does not replace official medical diagnostic instruments or professional hearing tests, and it must not be used as the basis for clinical diagnosis, prescription, or direct adjustment of a cochlear implant MAP (For research and educational purposes only).
- **Ethical and regulatory compliance in human-subject research:** A disclaimer alone does not automatically exempt the tool from medical device laws. To use it in research with actual participants, review by an Institutional Review Board (IRB) and written consent are required, and if the use qualifies as a clinical trial, check separately whether prior approval from the relevant authorities (such as Korea's Ministry of Food and Drug Safety) is required.
- **Results are the combined value of the person + the sound processor (external part of the cochlear implant) + the sound path + the device.**
  - Participants may not know their own sound processor settings.
  - Measurements made with different listening methods (speaker · cable · Bluetooth) should be analyzed separately.
- **Limits of the 18 Hz keep-alive signal (`tone18all`):**
  - Because an 18 Hz sine wave (peak −45 dBFS, RMS −48 dBFS) is added continuously from `master` even during trials, the 0.25 s silence before the first sound and the 0.5 s gaps between intervals are not pure digital zero (it is identical under all three intervals, so it gives no cue to the answer, and it is 22 dB below the stimulus RMS of −26 dBFS).
  - The sound processor's internal preprocessing (AGC, noise reduction, etc.) and Bluetooth codecs are black boxes, so there is no proof that the 18 Hz keep-alive signal has no effect at all. Therefore, **sessions with different keep-alive modes (e.g., v1 without keep-alive vs `tone18all` vs manually turned off by the user) are not combined but compared separately**.
- **Levels are not set in dB SPL.** Do not compare values directly with the 65 dB sound-field measurements in the literature, and do not rank participants against literature values.
- **Distinguish the nature of each value.**
  - A value from only one round is provisional.
  - “Not measured” is not a number.
  - “At chance” and “Incomplete” are reference values.
  - The results screen shows an average only when there are two or more numbers and all of them are normal values.
- **“Not measured” and “At chance” do not mean the person cannot hear the modulation.** They mean there is not enough evidence that the person discriminated it with this method.
- **Participants are told to press “Not sure” only when they really do not know.** Pressing it often does not make the record invalid.

## 6. Verification

`tools\전수검산.bat` (= `python -I -X utf8 tools/run_checks.py`) runs the following steps in order. If a step fails, the later steps are skipped.

1. **Stimulus · staircase JS = Python**
   - The output of the app's `stim.js` · `track.js`, run as-is with deno, is compared with `tools/tmtf_ref.py`, which was written separately without using the app's code.
   - 750 stimuli: RMS · peak · phase · samples
   - 210 staircases: depth · reversals · threshold · SD · not measured · at-chance verdict
2. **Screen code check**
   - `tools/app_check.js` runs everything from setup to the result file and the received-results viewer, using a simulated screen, a simulated AudioContext, and a fast clock.
   - It uses a model of a listener.
   - What it checks: the timing and duration of the three sounds, the modulation in the target position, levels, screen hiding, pausing, one more round, the 3 dB rule, files, CSVs, storage, and that no requests are sent
3. **Rebuilding the result file**
   - `tools/verify_result.py` rebuilds, in separately written Python, the result file that the screen saved in step 2.
   - It checks the staircase and the sound of each interval (seed → RMS · peak · phase).
4. **Lean 4 build** — `tools/lean`
   - Builds from scratch the theorems on level matching, SAM, ramps, band, grid, staircase, random numbers, spectrum, and the at-chance verdict.
5. **Lean axiom audit**
   - Checks that there is no `sorry` and no axioms beyond the standard ones (propext · Classical.choice · Quot.sound).
6. **JS ↔ Lean comparison**
   - Compares random numbers · ramps · bin ranges · band shape · interval lengths · grid · staircase · chance p line by line.
   - Also tests that deliberately altered lines are caught.

When everything passes, the hashes of the app files are recorded in `검증_통과.json` (the verification record).

- The page computes the hashes of the files it received when first opened and of the files on the server when a session starts. It compares them with the verification record and writes the result into the session's `build` (received version · server version · differing files).
- **Only when a new version is released** (when the app version number differs) does the page refuse to start and ask for a refresh.
- If only the hashes differ, the test is not blocked; the difference is only recorded. This is because some computers alter page files in transit (for example, when security software injects code into the page).
  - If the code files (js · css) match the verified ones and only `index.html` differs, it is recorded as `pass-code`.
  - Version 1.0.0 blocked the start whenever hashes differed, so it was always blocked in Chrome on some computers. This was fixed in 1.0.1.

## 7. References

1. Levitt, H. (1971). Transformed up-down methods in psychoacoustics. *The Journal of the Acoustical Society of America*, 49(2B), 467–477. https://doi.org/10.1121/1.1912375
   - 2-down 1-up adaptive staircase (Table I Entry 2, converging on 70.7%) and the principle of averaging an even number of reversals.
2. Viemeister, N. F. (1979). Temporal modulation transfer functions based upon modulation thresholds. *The Journal of the Acoustical Society of America*, 66(5), 1364–1380. https://doi.org/10.1121/1.383531
   - Sinusoidal amplitude modulation (SAM) of broadband noise and the definition of the TMTF, the correction for the increase in average power (Appendix, `√(1 + m²/2)`), and the low-frequency characteristics of gated carriers (§IV).
3. Won, J. H., Drennan, W. R., Nie, K., Jameyson, E. M., & Rubinstein, J. T. (2011). Acoustic temporal modulation detection and speech perception in cochlear implant listeners. *The Journal of the Acoustical Society of America*, 130(1), 376–388. https://doi.org/10.1121/1.3592521
   - Acoustic TMTF in cochlear implant listeners, 10 ms linear ramps, the staircase rule (start at 0 dB · 4 → 2 dB, switching after 4 reversals · mean of the last 10 of 14 reversals), and the shortened method with a third measurement when the difference exceeds 3 dB (Fig. 9).
4. Shen, Y., & Richards, V. M. (2013). Temporal modulation transfer function for efficient assessment of auditory temporal resolution. *The Journal of the Acoustical Society of America*, 133(2), 1031–1042. https://doi.org/10.1121/1.4773271
   - The SAM formula in Eq. (13), uniformly random modulation starting phase in `[0, 2π)` for each interval/trial, and normalization that removes level cues.
5. Park, M.-H., Won, J. H., Horn, D. L., & Rubinstein, J. T. (2015). Acoustic temporal modulation detection in normal-hearing and cochlear implanted listeners: Effects of hearing mechanism and development. *Journal of the Association for Research in Otolaryngology*, 16(3), 389–399. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10162-014-0499-z
   - A broadband Gaussian noise carrier newly generated for each interval, and the main procedure using the Won (2011) staircase rule with an additional third measurement when the difference exceeds 3 dB.
6. De Ruiter, A. M., Debruyne, J. A., Chenault, M. N., Francart, T., & Brokx, J. P. L. (2015). Amplitude modulation detection and speech recognition in late-implanted prelingually and postlingually deafened cochlear implant users. *Ear and Hearing*, 36(5), 557–566. https://doi.org/10.1097/AUD.0000000000000162
   - The three-interval oddity task, the 0.5 s stimulus · 0.5 s gap structure, and floor handling when modulation is not detected (this app extends it with “stop after 4 errors at 0 dB” and exclusion from averages).
7. Goehring, T., & Carlyon, R. P. (2026). A web-based listening test system for cochlear implant research and its validation for remote testing. *Trends in Hearing*, 30, 23312165261416179. https://doi.org/10.1177/23312165261416179
